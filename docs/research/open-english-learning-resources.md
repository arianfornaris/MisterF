# Open and Public-Domain Resources for Learning English

Legal, pedagogical, and product options for a new book, workbook, or course experience in Mister F.

**Prepared for Mister F**

July 13, 2026

> This research is informational and does not replace legal advice for international distribution.

## Executive Summary

> **Primary recommendation:** before building a complete course system, use these materials to create short tasks or exercises that appear on the home page as an alternative starting point for self-directed users. This first phase can validate formats, interests, and progression. Later, successful tasks can be organized into an original Mister F course supported by CC0, CC BY, and public-domain materials.

The easiest genuinely public-domain English-learning materials to locate are largely grammar books from the early twentieth century. Their legal status in the United States is favorable, but their language, cultural context, and teaching methods require substantial revision.

The strongest contemporary foundations are open educational resources (OER) under CC0 or CC BY and courses produced by United States federal agencies. These options permit commercial adaptation, combination, and distribution as long as each license and any author-noted exceptions are respected.

### Key Findings

- VOA *Let's Learn English* is the most complete reference for a sequential course. It includes stories, video, vocabulary, speaking, writing, printable worksheets, assessments, and teacher plans.
- *A Digital Workbook for Beginning ESOL* is the strongest model for a leveled digital workbook with self-checking exercises.
- *Home and School* and *Portland People and Places* offer straightforward patterns for graded reading, comprehension, and conversation under CC BY 4.0.
- The first product does not need to be a complete course. A curated set of home-page microtasks can give self-directed visitors an immediate, low-commitment action.
- A commercial product should avoid CC BY-NC and CC BY-ND. CC BY-SA is viable but adds obligations to shared adaptations.
- Provenance and licensing should be recorded per item—text, image, audio, and video—not only per book or web page.

## 1. Legal and Licensing Framework

“Free” does not necessarily mean “reusable.” A resource may be available at no cost while retaining all rights. An adaptable commercial product must distinguish public-domain works, openly licensed works, and restricted free access.

**Table 1. Practical comparison of rights categories**

| Category | Copy and adapt | Commercial use | Implication for Mister F |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public domain / CC0 | Yes | Yes | Maximum flexibility. Attribution is often advisable even when it is not required. |
| CC BY | Yes, with attribution | Yes | Preferred option for integrating and adapting modern content. |
| CC BY-SA | Yes, with attribution | Yes | A shared adaptation must retain a compatible license. |
| CC BY-NC | Only under noncommercial conditions | No | Unsuitable for a paid or subscription product without additional permission. |
| CC BY-ND | Redistribution without adaptation | May be permitted | Of limited use when translation, modification, or conversion into activities is required. |
| Free access | Usually no | Usually no | The resource may be linked to or studied, but not copied without a clear license or permission. |

Creative Commons recommends recording the title, author, source, and license—the TASL model—and indicating whether modifications were made. A general license may also include “except where otherwise noted”; every exception must then be reviewed separately.

The United States Copyright Office explains that ideas, methods, and systems are not protected by copyright, although their specific expression is protected. Mister F may study the pedagogical structure of a commercial book, but it may not reproduce that book's wording, illustrations, audio, or creative exercises.

### 2.1 VOA *Let's Learn English*

This is the closest candidate to the envisioned class-based course. Level 1 offers 52 beginner lessons, and Level 2 offers 30 intermediate lessons. Lessons combine narrative video, conversation, vocabulary, speaking, writing, printable worksheets, assessments, and teacher materials.

VOA permits educational and commercial reuse, with credit, of texts, audio, and video produced exclusively by VOA. Its policy also warns that certain photographs, news items, and third-party agency materials are not in the public domain.

> **Suggested decision:** use VOA's architecture and progression as the primary reference. Import specific content only after confirming that each asset is original VOA production.

### 2.2 Workbook and Graded-Reading Models

*A Digital Workbook for Beginning ESOL* demonstrates how to organize three levels, grammar units, readings, and self-checking exercises. *Home and School* and *Portland People and Places* demonstrate a structure especially appropriate for Mister F: a short reading, comprehension check, and conversation prompt.

These resources can be used commercially under CC BY 4.0, but the license should not be assumed to cover every linked video, photograph, or external resource. Adaptations must credit the source and identify the changes made.

## 3. Public-Domain Books

Project Gutenberg identifies the following titles as public domain in the United States and makes them available for free download. They are useful as secondary sources for exercises and grammar sequences, but they should not be republished without pedagogical and cultural modernization.

**Table 3. Older books with reuse potential**

| Title | Useful content | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *How to Speak and Write Correctly* | Vocabulary, grammar, sentences, punctuation, letters, errors, and style. | Broad source, but prescriptive in tone and dated in its examples. |
| *Practical Grammar and Composition* | Grammar, composition, and exercises for adults. | Useful practice bank; requires modern linguistic review. |
| *Higher Lessons in English* | Sentence structure, grammar, and systematic composition. | Useful for sequences and cumulative exercises. |
| *An Advanced English Grammar with Exercises* | Reference grammar and an extensive exercise collection. | Suitable as an advanced practice bank, not as a communicative experience. |
| *Business English: A Practice Book* | Oral communication, letters, advertising, banking, and business. | Potentially useful for professional English, but its commercial contexts are very old. |

Project Gutenberg's label refers to public-domain status in the United States. An internationally available application must verify status in other jurisdictions. CC0 and CC BY 4.0 provide a clearer international foundation than relying only on a work's publication date.

## 4. Product Proposal for Mister F

The new experience should be treated as a native structured resource rather than an embedded PDF. The same authored material could be presented as a navigable book, interactive workbook, sequential course, teacher guide, or set of independent tasks.

### 4.1 Immediate Opportunity: Home-Page Microtasks

Before implementing courses, Mister F can turn part of this material into short, independent tasks or exercises. These activities would appear on the home page as an alternative starting point for people who discover the platform on their own and do not yet have an assigned task, teacher, or clearly defined goal.

This approach lowers the initial barrier. Instead of asking the learner to select a course or configure a complex experience, the home page offers a concrete action that can be completed in a few minutes. After completion, the platform can recommend a related task, a tutor conversation, or a more substantial resource.

#### Recommended Characteristics

- Approximately 3–10 minutes in duration.
- One clearly identifiable communicative or linguistic objective.
- Immediate start with little or no prior configuration.
- Instructions understandable without teacher support.
- Useful feedback or a result upon completion.
- Level, skill, topic, and provenance recorded as metadata.
- A follow-up action: repeat, try a related task, practice with the tutor, or explore a complete resource.

#### Possible Initial Formats

- Short reading with comprehension questions.
- Vocabulary in context with meaning selection.
- Conversation ordering or reconstruction.
- Sentence correction or transformation.
- Brief written response to an everyday situation.
- Oral practice or roleplay based on a dialogue.
- Listening activity when the audio rights are clearly documented.

The open sources identified in this report can provide texts, ideas, and activity patterns, but every microtask must preserve the same rights traceability planned for courses. Whenever possible, Mister F should use the sources as pedagogical inspiration and write original content. If content is adapted or reproduced, its source, license, and modifications must be recorded.

#### Strategic Role

Microtasks do not necessarily replace the future course product. They serve as an earlier phase and a permanent discovery channel. They make it possible to observe which levels, topics, and formats drive starts, completions, repetitions, and continued engagement. The strongest tasks can later become components of units or structured learning paths.

### 4.2 Proposed Hierarchy for Future Courses

The base hierarchy is `Course > Unit > Lesson`. Each lesson contains the following components; review and cumulative assessment occur at the end of the unit.

- Objectives
- Story or dialogue
- Vocabulary
- Language focus
- Comprehension
- Guided practice
- Communicative task
- Writing or reflection
- Self-assessment

### 4.3 Recommended Lesson Template

1. **Define the communicative objective.** State what the learner will be able to do upon completion.
2. **Present brief input.** Use a story, dialogue, audio, video, or graded text.
3. **Activate vocabulary.** Provide minimal pre-teaching and contextual practice.
4. **Notice the language form.** Connect grammar or pronunciation to the input.
5. **Check comprehension.** Use graded questions and immediate feedback.
6. **Practice with support.** Use transformation, selection, ordering, or guided response.
7. **Produce language.** Use conversation, roleplay, brief writing, or an authentic task.
8. **Close with reflection.** Provide self-assessment, review, and a practice recommendation.

### 4.4 Minimum Viable Course

- An adult A1 course with 8–12 units.
- A recurring story or cast of characters for continuity.
- Four to six lessons per unit with cumulative review.
- Original Mister F readings, dialogues, and activities.
- Generated or recorded audio with clearly documented rights.
- Automatic attribution for every reused OER component.

## 5. Compliance by Design

The application should not make compliance dependent on remembering each source's conditions manually. Provenance must be part of the resource data model and every reused asset.

### 5.1 Minimum Metadata

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `sourceTitle` | Title of the original work or asset. |
| `sourceAuthor` | Author or organization that must receive credit. |
| `sourceUrl` | Permanent link to the original source. |
| `licenseCode` / `licenseVersion` | Exact license type and version. |
| `attributionText` | Attribution text ready for display. |
| `isModified` / `modificationNote` | Whether the work was adapted and how it changed. |
| `assetRights` | Rights specific to image, audio, video, or text. |
| `verifiedAt` | Date when the license was checked. |

### 5.2 Recommended Controls

- Default allowlist: public domain, CC0, and CC BY.
- Explicit warning before creating an adaptation under CC BY-SA.
- Import blocking for NC, ND, or All Rights Reserved unless documented permission exists.
- Automatic Sources and Attributions page per course, unit, and asset.
- Exception records when an open book contains external material not covered by its general license.
- Visible distinction between original, adapted, unmodified reproduced, and linked-only content.

## 6. Recommended Plan

1. **Define the initial audience.** Self-directed users, beginning with A1 adults and everyday communicative goals.
2. **Implement traceability.** Add metadata, attribution, and license controls before importing or adapting content.
3. **Create a pilot microtask collection.** Test several formats, topics, and skills on the home page.
4. **Measure behavior.** Observe starts, completions, repetitions, and continuation into another task, the tutor, or a resource.
5. **Select effective patterns.** Identify which tasks work as entry points and which can be grouped pedagogically.
6. **Create the scope and sequence.** Design an original progression using results, VOA, and OER as references rather than copying them literally.
7. **Prototype one unit.** Reuse the strongest tasks within a story, four lessons, a review, and an assessment.
8. **Scale the catalog.** Add units, levels, and thematic courses after validating both formats.

> **Final selection:** first product step: home-page microtasks for self-directed users. Future curricular foundation: VOA *Let's Learn English*. Workbook model: *A Digital Workbook for Beginning ESOL*. Initial readings: *Home and School*. Core content: original Mister F authorship. Preferred licenses: public domain, CC0, and CC BY.
