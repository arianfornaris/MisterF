# Scene Script Level Guide

Use this guide when authoring scripts for scene images.

## A1-A2

- Keep the script around 35-70 words.
- Use mostly present simple, present continuous, simple past, and common modals.
- Prefer concrete nouns, visible actions, emotions, times, and places.
- Use short dialogue turns.
- Avoid idioms, long clauses, and abstract explanation.

## B1-B2

- Keep the script around 80-130 words.
- Add reasons, reactions, plans, and mild negotiation.
- Use connectors such as `because`, `although`, `after`, `before`, `so`, and `while`.
- Include useful classroom vocabulary, but keep the situation easy to infer from the image.
- Dialogue can sound natural, but should remain clean and classroom-safe.

## C1

- Keep the script around 130-190 words.
- Add nuance, implied meaning, repair strategies, and more natural social language.
- Use richer verbs, embedded clauses, and varied sentence rhythm.
- Keep the story grounded in the image. Do not invent unrelated backstory.
- Avoid slang that would make the audio less reusable for international learners.

## General Rules

- The script must support the image, not replace it.
- Do not mention panel numbers in the transcript.
- Do not describe text that does not exist in the image.
- Prefer clear audio comprehension over literary style.
- Each script should stand alone as listening input after the learner sees the image.
