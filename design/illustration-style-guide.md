# Mister F Illustration Style Guide

This guide defines the shared illustration direction for pre-generated visual assets across the Mister F platform.

## Core Direction

Mister F illustrations should feel like modern English workbook artwork: friendly, clear, expressive, and easy to describe.

The target style is:

- clean 2D cartoon illustration;
- warm educational tone;
- simple readable shapes;
- expressive faces and body language;
- soft but minimal shading;
- inclusive, varied characters;
- classroom-safe everyday situations;
- no photorealism, watercolor realism, heavy texture, or painterly rendering.

The existing roleplay avatars are the primary style reference. Scene images should look like they belong in the same product family, even when they include backgrounds, props, or multiple characters.

## Character Style

- Use rounded, approachable proportions.
- Keep facial features simple and readable at small sizes.
- Use natural, varied skin tones, ages, body types, hairstyles, and clothing.
- Avoid stereotypes, caricatures, or costume-like cultural shortcuts.
- Make emotions visible through eyebrows, eyes, mouth shape, posture, and hand position.
- Keep recurring characters visually consistent across panels within the same image.

## Scene Style

- Favor everyday places: cafes, streets, classrooms, stores, homes, clinics, parks, offices, buses, and stations.
- Keep backgrounds simple enough that the learning task remains obvious.
- Include only the props needed to understand the story.
- Use clear staging: the learner should understand who is doing what without needing text.
- Avoid clutter, tiny important objects, or ambiguous action.

## Color And Rendering

- Use bright but natural colors.
- Prefer flat or semi-flat color fills with light, clean shading.
- Use crisp outlines or clean edge definition.
- Avoid dramatic lighting, dark cinematic palettes, noisy texture, and washed-out realism.
- Keep contrast high enough for thumbnail readability.

## Text Policy

Default rule: no readable text inside generated illustrations.

Avoid:

- captions;
- speech bubbles;
- signs;
- labels;
- visible brand marks;
- readable menus, posters, papers, books, screens, or receipts;
- letters or numbers used as decoration.

Only allow text when a specific exercise requires reading that exact text. In those cases, the metadata entry must mark the image as text-dependent.

## Story Images

The default scene image format is a four-panel wordless story.

Use a clean 2x2 comic grid:

- Panel 1: setup;
- Panel 2: problem or change;
- Panel 3: reaction or attempt;
- Panel 4: resolution or next-step cue.

The four panels should show different moments of the same situation, not four unrelated examples. The learner should be able to write or tell a short story from left to right, top to bottom.

## Quality Bar

Before approving an image, confirm that:

- it matches the platform 2D cartoon style;
- it has no unwanted readable text;
- the four-panel sequence is understandable;
- character identity remains consistent across panels;
- the central action is clear at 720x720;
- it supports at least one real classroom activity;
- it is inclusive and classroom-safe.
